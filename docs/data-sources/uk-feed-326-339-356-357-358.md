## #357: Microcosm's microcosm#1095 ports

### 1. HMRC Annual Stamp Tax Statistics 2024 to 2025

The corrected tables of 11 March 2026 (first released 11 December 2025):
`Annual_Stamps_2024_25_correction.ods`, sha256 `ee391638…`, 395,545 bytes. The correction
changed only the NRSDLT-overlap percentages in Tables 6b and 6c (column I, 22 cells), which
are not ported; every ported cell is identical in the December file.

- **Table 8a (residential) and 8b (non-residential)**, both financial years in the workbook
  (2023-24 and 2024-25): transactions, estimated property value and receipts, by price band,
  for individuals, other buyers and all buyers. HMRC's sheets say "other buyers"; its
  commentary (section 8) calls them non-natural persons: companies, partnerships and
  collective investment schemes. 2024-25 residential: other buyers paid £1,230m of £10,380m;
  non-residential: £3,260m of £3,505m.
- Table 8a's "Corporate bodies paying flat rate" row lies outside the price bands (rows 9 to
  15 partition row 8) and has no individuals ([X]).
- **Table 6b**, transactions paying the higher rates on additional dwellings (HRAD), in total
  and by ten price bands: transactions, property value, HRAD receipts (gross) and the
  surcharge element. Both receipts columns are before HRAD refunds (row 5 note; row 3's
  "net" contradicts the header and row 5). The table has no buyer split and covers
  individuals and companies alike. The OBR's HRAD line is net: EFO 3.7's £2.29bn for 2024-25
  matches the workbook's Table 2f net surcharge (£2,292m), not Table 6b's gross £2,790m.
- Not ported: Table 6b's percentage columns, its "Residential receipts" column (restating
  Table 8a and 6a) and its corporate row (only that column is published).

Semantics on every fact:
- geography `hmrc:england_and_northern_ireland` (SDLT applies there; publication page and
  commentary section 3.1), level `statistical_scope`;
- the constraint `sdlt_chargeable_consideration >= 40000` (each sheet's coverage note);
- financial years by opening year; the vintage names the corrected edition;
- rounding as published (transactions to 100, values and receipts to £5m), so bands need not
  sum to the total; `[X]` (fewer than 50 transactions, under £2.5m or not available) emits
  no fact.

### 2. The corporate share of SDLT in OBR's forecast years

The Economic and fiscal outlook publishes **no split of SDLT receipts by type of buyer**, and
no corporate SDLT projection. Checked: the March 2026 EFO's detailed forecast tables
(receipts sheets 3.7 and 3.8), the supplementary fiscal tables, monthly profiles, devolved
tables, policy measures database, the June 2026 Forecast evaluation report and the July 2026
Fiscal risks and sustainability report. HMRC's buyer-type tables (8a, 8b) are outturn only.

The EFO's only SDLT breakdown is sheet 3.7, "Property transactions taxes: Receipts by
sector", by property type and rate. policyengine-uk#2322 (a pull request) projects the
corporate statistic by holding HMRC's 2024-25 other-buyer share within each property type
and applying it to 3.7's residential and non-residential lines. So Chronicle now carries
sheet 3.7's SDLT lines in `obr-efo-receipts-march-2026`, 2024-25 to 2030-31: residential,
its main rates, HRAD and non-UK resident surcharge, and non-residential. Since
chronicle#397 the March package keeps their 2024-25 outturn, and the October 2026 EFO
package (`obr-efo-receipts-october-2026`, table 4.7) carries 2025-26 on under the same
line ids. Total SDLT (3.7 row
6) is not repeated: it is sheet 3.8's `obr.sdlt`, which equals residential plus
non-residential. Carrying the share forward is Microcosm's declared rule.

### 3. Move to UC claim rates

`dwp-mtuc-households-legacy-benefit-move-status-july-2022-june-2026` (80 facts) comes from
Stat-Xplore's `MtUC_Households`, extracted 15 October 2026. It counts households sent a
migration notice by "Legacy Benefit" (19 detailed combinations) and "Move Status" (claimed UC;
did not claim UC, legacy benefit closed; in progress), with DWP's own totals, for Great
Britain.

- **It is cumulative, as DWP advises.** Stat-Xplore adds random error to every cell. DWP's
  background information advises taking a statistic from the tabulation that carries it
  rather than summing finer cells. So the query omits the notice-month field, and each cell
  covers every notice month from July 2022 to June 2026. The period is month 2026-06, the
  latest notice month; `period_coverage` holds the window.
- **It is the table policyengine-uk-data#534 used.** Of all households, 1,822,966 were sent a
  notice: 1,580,761 claimed, 241,662 did not and 538 are in progress. Claimed over claimed
  plus did not claim (1,822,423, the rate policyengine-uk-data#534 draws) is 86.74%; DWP's
  87% divides by every notice (1,822,966), 86.71%. The two part as in-progress cases grow.
  DWP's Universal Credit quarterly statistics of 18 August 2026 report the same release: 1.8
  million households sent a notice to end June 2026, 87% claimed.
- **Outcomes follow DWP's definitions.** A claim counts if made within one month after the
  deadline or any extension, so some claims made after the legacy award closed count as
  claimed. In progress means the deadline has not passed. Statuses are as at DWP's extract.
- **Status and vintages.** Every fact's evidence notes open with "Official statistics in
  development", and its vintage is the extraction date. Earlier releases cannot be
  recovered:
  - the gov.uk workbooks (August 2023 to August 2024) split only DWP benefits against tax
    credits only, and the releases since November 2024 are HTML only;
  - from August 2026 the series is a summary inside the UC quarterly release;
  - Stat-Xplore serves only the current release (next update 17 November 2026).

  Later extracts can land as packages beside this one.
- **Not ported:** the "Legacy non-customer" row and the "Unknown/Missing" status, which are
  zero in every cell. Other zeros are kept as published.

### 4. The geography of OBR table 4.9's DWP lines

Table 4.9 prints no geography. Its "DWP social security" lines are DWP's forecast: DWP's
benefit expenditure and caseload tables (Spring 2026, the
`dwp-benefit-expenditure-caseload-spring-2026` package) say in Notes 1 that they match this
EFO and that table 4.9 publishes the DWP forecast, and Notes 3 and 4 give the coverage. Each
line now carries it (no value changes):

| line | geography |
|---|---|
| housing benefit (not on JSA), incapacity benefits, Pension Credit, statutory maternity pay, both Universal Credit lines, State Pension, jobseeker's allowance | Great Britain (K03000001), with people resident overseas |
| DLA and PIP, Attendance Allowance (moved 1 April 2020), Carer's Allowance (3 September 2018) | England and Wales (K04000001), every year the table carries |
| Winter Fuel Payment (devolved April 2024) | England and Wales from 2024-25, the table's first year |

new stamps. microcosm#1100 already applies the same scopes to the modelled side.

### 5. HBAI, FYE 2025, with confidence intervals

The FYE 1995 to FYE 2025 release (26 March 2026):
- `dwp-hbai-summary-fye2025`, from the summary results workbook: Table 1.2b's "Quintile 3
  (median)", equivalised weekly household income in average 2024/25 prices, and Table 1.3a's
  percentage of individuals in relative low income (below 60 per cent of the same year's
  median), before and after housing costs, for every year 1994/95 to 2024/25 (124 facts).
  FYE 2025: median £719 BHC and £623 AHC a week; 15.9 and 19.6 per cent in relative low
  income.
- `dwp-hbai-uncertainty-fye2025`, from `uncertainty-hbai-2024-25-tables.ods` inside the
  release's data-table ZIP (the only place DWP publishes them): Tables 8a and 8b's 95%
  confidence bounds for those four FYE 2025 statistics, on a `statistic` dimension
  (`ci_95_lower_bound`, `ci_95_upper_bound`), stored values (the sheets display them rounded).
  Median BHC £703.6 to £735.6. The new `zip_ods_used_range` parser reads the member.

Semantics: the release is the vintage on every fact; medians carry
`price_basis: average_2024_25_prices` because each release re-prices the series; figures are
UK from 2002/03 and Great Britain before (Notes), so those years carry Great Britain; a break
in series sits between 2020/21 and 2021/22 (Note 1); the equivalisation scale is modified
OECD (AHC companion scale after housing costs); intervals are bootstrapped from the FRS design
(methodology report). DWP publishes intervals only for each release's latest year. DWP plans
to revise FYE 2025 in March 2027; that release is a new vintage.

### 1. DESNZ Northern Ireland electricity and gas

Both workbooks, every year sheet and measure (published 18 December 2025):

| | electricity 2009 to 2024 | gas 2015 to 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| scopes | domestic standard, Economy 7, all domestic, all non-domestic, all meters (from 2015) | domestic, non-domestic, all meters |
| measures | meters (thousands), consumption (GWh), mean and (from 2015) median kWh per meter | meters, consumption, mean (no median) |

| rows | NI (includes unallocated), 11 districts (26 pre-2015 districts before 2015, under the workbook's NUTS/LAU codes), Unallocated | NI and 11 districts |

The all-domestic meter count and consumption use the GB package's concepts, filters and
measure ids. DESNZ cautions that Northern Ireland's figures are not directly comparable with
Great Britain's: gas classes meters by profile marker, not by GB's 73,200 kWh threshold
(methodology booklet, July 2026, sections 4.2 and 4.3), and electricity differs in market
structure and collection (section 5.3). Northern Ireland's electricity rows also file three
`domestic_scope` values under the domestic concept where Great Britain's file one. Every NI
measure's evidence notes carry both caveats; how far the two can be combined is Microcosm's
declared decision. 2024: 848,085
domestic electricity meters and 2,695.7 GWh; 322,974 domestic gas meters and 2,985.0 GWh. Gas
`[no data]` cells (Fermanagh and Omagh and Mid Ulster, non-domestic and all meters, from
2019) and blank means (no meters of the type) emit no fact.

### 2. The GB gas year

DESNZ publishes no exact dates for Great Britain's gas years: the cover sheets, the
methodology booklet (section 2 and Table 2) and the 2024 summary report all say mid-May to
mid-May (since 2018). Only Northern Ireland's are dated (methodology section 4.1, page 32:
15 May to 15 May from 2018; 1 October 2014 to 30 September 2015 for 2015). So Chronicle adds
a period type, `gas_year`, for DESNZ's gas year under its publisher label, and stamps:
- the GB local-authority gas rows (`desnz.subnational.gas.cy2023`, `.cy2024`, record set ids
  kept so source record ids stay stable) with no dates;
- the NI gas rows and the LSOA gas rows likewise, NI with its published dates.

The period type changes these facts' identity; values do not change. GB electricity keeps its
February-to-January coverage (cover sheets, Annex B); note that the methodology's Table 4
dates the 2024 non-half-hourly year 31 January 2024 to 30 January 2025.

### 3. DESNZ sub-local domestic energy

Per the ruling (meters and kWh at the finest grain), `desnz-lsoa-domestic-gas-consumption-2024`
and `desnz-lsoa-domestic-electricity-consumption-2024` carry the number of domestic meters and
total domestic consumption (kWh) for 2023 and 2024: the additive measures Microcosm sums to
constituencies through its best-fit supports.
- Geography: 33,755 English and 1,917 Welsh LSOAs (2021 boundaries) and 7,392 Scottish data
  zones (2022 boundaries) in the same workbook, plus its "Unallocated" row
  (`desnz:great_britain_unallocated`). There are no separate Scottish files: the LSOA files
  include data zones and the MSOA files intermediate zones.
- Suppression: no text markers; areas with no domestic meters, or whose meters went to
  Unallocated for disclosure control (fewer than 5 meters, or the top two over 90 per cent),
  are blank and emit no fact (gas: 1,608 and 1,605 rows; electricity: 5 and 2).
- DESNZ advises against aggregating these figures to local authority, region or country
  (cover sheets); the GB gas meter count is consuming meters only.
- Not ported: mean and median per meter (the mean is consumption over meters; medians do not
  add), the gas non-consuming meters, and 2010 to 2022.
- The MSOA/intermediate-zone workbooks are registered raw-only
  (`desnz-msoa-domestic-{gas,electricity}-consumption-2010-2024`), their bytes in R2 only.

**Storage.** The LSOA workbooks are 55.6 MB and 50.7 MB. Their bytes are in R2 only
(`storage.local: false`, git-ignored); R2 keeps the archive copy, which needs credentials to
read. CI sets `LEDGER_SOURCE_ARTIFACT_FETCH=1` and, before any test,
`scripts/fetch_r2_only_source_artifacts.py` fetches each from its gov.uk `source_url`
(retrying on transport errors and 5xx answers), checks the pinned sha256 and caches it
(`~/.cache/policyengine-chronicle/source-artifacts`, kept between runs by `actions/cache`),
so a gov.uk outage or a replaced file fails that step by name. Locally, set the variable or
place the file in that cache; without either, the tests that need a workbook skip, and the
package builder reads it through `read_registered_artifact`, never straight from `db/data`.
The three largest raw-only registrations, which no build reads (the two MSOA workbooks and
the ONS rural-urban workbook), are R2-only too.

**Parsing.** The XLSX parser loads a workbook whole, twice, even when a package names two
sheets: over 11 minutes for one of these 15-sheet files. The new opt-in parser
`xlsx_read_only_used_range` streams only the named sheets (about 10 seconds) and emits the
same cells. It is opt-in because read-only mode differs from the full load on three existing
packages (different used ranges, and a whitespace-only cell read as text), so no existing
package changes. The streamed parse re-measures each sheet's used range rather than trust its
`<dimension>` element, which a writer can understate: a synthetic test with an understated
element and formula cells pins parity with the full load, and re-measuring leaves both LSOA
packages byte-identical.

### 5. Bus revenue by country

DfT publishes no country split of fare receipts or support: BUS05 is England only, and
BUS04ai, the only DfT table with Scotland, Wales and Great Britain, publishes total estimated
operating revenue alone (fares, concessionary reimbursement, BSOG and support combined).
`dft-bus04i-operating-revenue-2025` carries that line for Scotland, Wales and Great Britain,
years ending March 2024 and 2025 (Wales £172.8m and £203.7m), under the BUS05i concept; England
is BUS05ai's identical cell. Scotland's passenger revenue and support are Transport Scotland's
(Scottish Transport Statistics Table 2.8, GB and Scotland rows); no published source splits
Welsh revenue by type.

## #326: Scottish Census 2022 tenure for BRMA rent targets

The original ward × bedrooms table-builder export (`household2022ftb`, sha 03d6157d…) was not
found, and a new one would need a guest session accepting NRS's user agreement and would not
reproduce the pinned checksum. Per the ruling, Chronicle uses UV404 at output area, the
alternative #313 offers: `nrs-census2022-uv404-tenure-output-area`, from the UK Data Service
copy of the CSV (sha256 `d6abb0ee…`, byte for byte the member of NRS's output-area topic ZIP).
- Ported: "All occupied households" (column B) and NRS's own "Private rented: Total" (column
  I), for 46,363 output areas: 2,508,542 households and 322,903 private renters in the ported
  cells. The other tenure columns stay in the preserved file.
- Markers: 4,910 "Private rented: Total" cells print `-`. No NRS page or file defines it. NRS
  publishes small counts, zero included, under record swapping and cell key perturbation, and
  read as zero the output-area sums match the ward and council tables within perturbation
  noise, so it most likely means zero; Chronicle keeps the symbol in the source rows and
  emits no fact.
- Geography: NRS gives output areas no names; the code is the name as well as the id.
- The tenure-by-bedrooms joint distribution remains unavailable; nothing is synthesized.

## #339: Universal Credit households by area and Payment Indicator

Three packages re-extract the area series of 7 October 2026 from Stat-Xplore's
`UC_Households`, with "Payment Indicator" as a dimension. They run monthly from January 2025
to May 2026, still the latest month, and were extracted on 15 October 2026.

| package | facts |
|---|---:|
| `dwp-uc-households-by-constituency-payment-indicator-january-2025-may-2026` | 21,488 |
| `dwp-uc-households-by-local-authority-payment-indicator-january-2025-may-2026` | 11,900 |
| `dwp-uc-households-by-constituency-children-payment-indicator-january-2025-may-2026` | 150,416 |

- **Yes and No, with no total.** "Yes" is a household paid a calculated award in the month
  and "No" one with a nil award. Their sum is the all-claims count. Against the retired
  packages it agrees to within 9 households in every area-month (11 for constituency and
  child band), with a median absolute gap of 2 households (0 in 28% of constituency, child
  band and month cells). The gap is the random error Stat-Xplore adds to each cube.
- **Otherwise the semantics are unchanged.**
  - The geography and child-band recodes are the October query's.
  - There is one record set per month, indicator and child band.
  - The "Unknown" area and the pre-April-2019 child marker emit no facts.
  - Measure ids and concepts are unchanged, so Microcosm adds `payment_indicator: Yes` to
    its five area selectors. The four child-band selectors list `dimensions:
    ["number_of_children"]`, which must equal a fact's dimension names, so they also need
    `payment_indicator` in that list or they match no fact.
- **The three all-claims packages are retired**
  (`dwp-uc-households-by-{constituency,local-authority,constituency-children}-january-2025-may-2026`),
  as #313 retired the May 2025 snapshots.
  - Their aliases go, and their preserved bytes stay under `db/data`.
  - Otherwise a selector with no payment indicator would match both an all-claims cell and
    its two parts.
  - Their May 2025 legacy selector families (`uk.local_geography.uc_households.*`) leave the
    feed with them. Microcosm's selectors on main do not use them.
